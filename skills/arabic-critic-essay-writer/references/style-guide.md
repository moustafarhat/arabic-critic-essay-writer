# Style guide: Reflective Arabic Critic

Evidence base: one essay, about 2,500 words, 19 paragraphs, 84 sentences (embedded reader
comments were excluded). Everything below is observed unless marked *(inference)*.

## 1. Load-bearing traits (get these right and it already sounds like it)

1. **Long build, short landing.** Sentences are long and nested (mean about 30 words, half run
   30+, the longest 80), but each paragraph is punctuated by a blunt short sentence of 3-8 words
   (about 14% of sentences). The short one states the flat fact or the verdict on the long one.
   Examples of the type: "لا شيء من هذه أحداث درامية." / "الألبوم لا يقول هذا."
   Two or three short sentences may cluster at a turn, but never three paragraphs in a row.
2. **Define by negation, then correct.** The writer names the expected reading and rejects it
   before giving the real one: "ليس ... وإنما ...", "لا ... بل ...", "ليس لكي ... بل لكي ...".
   About 15 instances in 2,500 words, roughly one per 170 words. Sentences and paragraphs often
   open with لا / ليس / وهذا (together about 19% of openers). This is how thesis and nuance
   arrive: the cliché is named first so the sharper claim has something to push against.
3. **The work is an agent with intentions; the critic keeps score of what it says.** The work
   "bets" (يراهن), "insists" (يصر), "knows" (تدرك), "invites the listener to notice"
   (تتحدى المستمع). The critic then separates the work's claim from the critic's inference and
   flags the gap ("لا يتناوله الفيديو بالتفصيل، لكنني أعتقد ...", "الألبوم لا يقول هذا").
4. **Calibrated hedge plus precision word.** A claim gets one trimming qualifier and one
   sharpening word: يبدو / ربما / أو على الأقل / على أقل تقدير paired with تحديدًا / بالضبط /
   حرفيًا. Hedges attach to a specific claim and never float ("ربما هو المغزى", not "ربما").
   Also the frame "نوع من ..." (nine times) for naming a device or a mood without over-claiming.
5. **A central tension, named early and left open.** Paragraph 2-3 says what the piece is really
   about ("التوتر يكاد يكون جوهر الموضوع"). The ending returns to the opening paradox and
   declares it unresolvable and probably the point ("لا تُحل أبدًا، وهذا أمرٌ جيد").
6. **Homely image for an abstraction.** Once every two or three paragraphs, a domestic or
   bodily image carries the idea: the foundation of a house, boring and buried; drawing yourself
   because you are the only model awake at 3 a.m.; years piling up like sediment; an idea that
   sounds nice on a greeting card; a box the listener fills in. Images are plain, single and
   short, never stacked.

## 2. Voice and stance
- A critic thinking in public. Mostly impersonal narration; "I" surfaces through verb endings
  about six times in the essay, always at a pivot: a self-check ("أريد هنا أن أكون حذرًا من
  المبالغة في الدفاع عن ..."), a disclaimer ("لا أذكُر هذا كاكتشافٍ لتناقض"), an opinion
  ("أعتقد أن على الناقد المنصف أن ..."), a promise ("أرغب في التفكير فيه قبل الانتقال إلى ...").
- Attitude: sympathetic but unwilling to flatter; interested, slightly dry, fair. Admiration is
  shown by attention to small details, never by superlatives.
- The reader is a smart equal who may know the context. Occasional generic "you" for an
  aphorism or a condition ("أنت ترسم نفسك لأنك ...", "إذا كنتَ مُتابعًا للتاريخ ..."). The
  inclusive imperative appears once, to zoom out ("لننظر إلى الصورة الأوسع").
- Signposts the essay's own progress in a sentence when useful (a coming section, a question
  parked for later).

## 3. Sentence and rhythm
- Sentences: median about 28 words; 10th percentile about 6, 90th about 56. Mix, not average:
  several long sentences, then one short, occasionally a mid-length colon sentence.
- Paragraphs: 80-175 words (mean 133), 3-10 sentences. Solid blocks of prose. No headings, no
  lists, no bold, no one-line paragraphs.
- Punctuation per 1,000 words: commas about 88 (clause boundaries are visible), colons about 6
  (announce a reframing or a quoted formula: "المقصود هو: ..."), en dashes with spaces about 3.5
  (parenthetical qualification, "–"), parentheses about 1 (a dry aside), question marks about
  1.5, semicolons almost never, no exclamation marks, no ellipses.
- Connectors: و / ثم / إذن / وهذا / لكن / بل / إلا أن / بعبارة أخرى. "وهذا" extends a thought
  into a comment on it ("وهذا يثير سؤالًا ...", "وهذا، إن أردنا أن نكون صريحين، ...").
  Frequent inserted parentheticals between commas ("، في هذه المرحلة،", "، بحكم كونهم ...،").
- Long sentences are periodic: the point arrives late, after conditions and contrasts. Do not
  pad them with adjectives; the length comes from qualifications.

## 4. Diction and register
- Formal contemporary MSA, abstract and analytic (بُنية، مفارقة، سردية، وظيفة، عنصر نائب،
  محفّز)  with a few plain words for bite (ممل، محرج، فارغ). No slang in narration.
- Foreign or colloquial material stays inside curly quotes “ ” and is unpacked in the next
  clause. Latin-script titles are allowed inside quotes. Quoted speech is only about 2% of the
  words but quote marks are frequent (about 28 per 1,000 words), mostly for short phrases.
- Recurring function phrases (style, not topic): بمعنى آخر / بعبارة أخرى، أو على الأقل، في هذه
  المرحلة، يبدو أقل شبهًا بـ ... وأكثر شبهًا بـ ...، بدلًا من أن ...، في آنٍ واحد، على أقل
  تقدير، ثمة ... ، اللافت هو ...
- Tanween on alif is written "-ًا" (تحديدًا, أحيانًا). Partial tashkeel only where meaning could
  wobble (مُعدّ، يُذكرنا). Arabic comma ، and question mark ؟. Western digits for years and
  larger counts (2011, 37); small counts are spelled out (ثماني حلقات، أربعة أطفال).
- Avoided: marketing adjectives (رائع، مذهل، ملحمي), intensifiers (جدًا، للغاية) stacked on
  praise, moralising verbs (يجب، ينبغي) aimed at the reader.

## 5. Rhetoric and imagery
- Device inventory: contrast pairs (X not Y, but Z), the reframed cliché ("انظروا إلى أي مدى
  وصلت" vs "انظروا كم هو غريب أن ..."), the aphorism on the paragraph's last line, the
  unanswered question, the "form mirrors content" observation (the sound changed when the
  subject changed), the "axis, not position" move (a new period is not a third point on the same
  line but a change of line).
- Humor: dry, tucked into parentheses or a subordinate clause ("ومثير للغضب بعض الشيء، كما هو
  الحال مع أي شيء جاد حقًا"). About three times in the essay. Never a joke sentence of its own.
- Sound: no saj', no rhyme. Cadence comes from balanced clauses, not ornament.
- Word play is reported by the critic and then tested, not celebrated ("قد يبدو لطيفًا كفكرة
  على بطاقة معايدة، إلا أن ...").

## 6. Structure
- **Opening:** a comparison with a different work or a puzzling formal choice, then a pivot
  sentence that says why the comparison matters. Not a thesis statement, not a scene.
- **Paragraph shape:** (a) say what the work does or claims, using a small concrete detail;
  (b) complicate it (a hedge, a contrast, a fair objection); (c) end on a reframe, a question, or
  a dry pivot. Roughly a third of paragraphs end on a question or an open implication.
- **Middle:** at about the halfway mark, zoom out ("لننظر إلى الصورة الأوسع") and place the work
  in a history with periods and dates; treat the earlier periods as positions on an axis, then
  argue the new work changes the axis.
- **Fairness beat:** one paragraph raises the fact the work avoids (money, power, contradiction)
  and says the critic ought to mention it, then declines to turn it into an accusation.
- **Ending:** "وهذا يعيدنا إلى نقطة البداية". Restate the opening paradox, judge it "not resolved,
  and that is probably the point", and finish on one long, balanced image-sentence, often after a
  colon, that leaves the tension standing. No call to action, no moral, no thanks.

## 7. Content moves
- Read the work through one or two small, specific details (a date, a cup of coffee, a mother
  named outright instead of alluded to) and let them carry the argument; the grand claim is
  earned by the small case.
- Anticipate the obvious reading and dismiss it in a clause before offering the better one.
- Invert flattering narratives (success as proof for someone else, not pride for oneself).
- Ask what happens when the work's own mechanism fails (the songs stop working too).
- Attribute darker statements to another voice when that is what the work does, and say so.
- Trust the reader: the critic does not explain a detail twice, and leaves an implication
  unsaid with "الألبوم لا يقول هذا. ليس بالضرورة أن يكون الأمر كذلك."

## 8. Never-list
- No headings, bullets, numbered lists, bold, emojis or tables inside an essay.
- No exclamation marks, no ellipses, no rhetorical pileups of questions.
- No superlative praise or star-rating language; no "must-listen / must-see" verdicts.
- No plot or tracklist summary without an argument attached to each item.
- No slang or dialect in the critic's own narration (only inside quotation marks).
- No moral, call to action, "في الختام", or neat resolution at the end.
- No invented quotes, lyrics or facts to make a point; no more than a phrase of quoted text at a time.
- No stacked metaphors: one image per idea, plain and domestic.
- Do not open every paragraph with the same connector; do not begin two consecutive
  paragraphs with a negation.

## 9. Dosage (per 1,000 words, about)
- Negation-then-correction constructions: 5-6.
- Short landing sentences (3-8 words): 1 per paragraph on average, never more than 2 in a row.
- First-person pivots: 2-3. Rhetorical or open questions: 1.5-2.
- Homely images: 2-3. Dry parenthetical asides: 1.
- Hedge words (يبدو، ربما، على الأقل): 6-8 in total. Precision words (تحديدًا، بالضبط): 3-4.
- Quoted phrases in “ ”: about 12-14 (the source has about 28 quote marks per 1,000 words, mostly
  short phrases). If the subject has no words worth quoting, use quotes for named formulas,
  coined terms and the cliché being rejected (“انظروا إلى أي مدى وصلت”-type phrases).
- One fairness paragraph per essay. One zoom-out paragraph per essay.

## 9b. Drift watch-list (found while testing on unrelated topics)
Natural first drafts drift in the same directions. Correct them on purpose:
- **Sentences too short** (about 20 words instead of 30). Fuse two neighbouring claims with
  لأن / بينما / أما / وهي, and hang a qualifier ("في هذه الحالة", "بمعناه المعتاد") inside the
  sentence. Keep the short landing sentences; lengthen the rest.
- **Paragraphs too thin** (about 100 words). Aim for about 130; the source rarely goes below 100.
- **Semicolons and colons overused.** Semicolons: 0-1 per essay. Colons: about 2 per 400 words, each
  announcing a reframing or a formula. Use commas, و or a full stop instead.
- **Too many questions.** Two in 400 words is already above the source. One central question plus
  at most one more.
- **No quotation marks.** Add short quoted phrases (see Dosage); a draft with none feels bare.
- **No dry asides.** Add one parenthetical or en-dash aside per 300-400 words.
- **Standalone first-person pronouns.** The source carries "I" and "we" in verb endings
  (أعتقد، أريد، أرغب); it never writes أنا. Avoid stand-alone pronouns.
- **Image pile-up.** Two images in one paragraph is the ceiling; one is better.
- **Signature overdose.** Drafts that "try hard" put a ليس ... بل and a hedge in almost every
  sentence (13+ negation frames and 19 hedges per 1,000 words in testing, against 5-6 and 6-8 in
  the source). Keep negation-then-correction for the claims that matter, and let plain assertive
  sentences carry the rest. `style_stats.py check` flags this as "too many".

## 10. Genre adaptations
Observed genre: long-form cultural criticism about a music release. *(All below is inference.)*
- Film, book, series, exhibition reviews: carry over everything; swap "listener" for viewer or
  reader; keep the central-tension shape.
- Opinion or general essays on a social habit: keep the negation-then-correction spine, the
  hedges and images; the work-as-agent trait becomes "the habit / the institution as agent".
- Shorter pieces (600-1,000 words): one zoom-out sentence instead of a paragraph, one image, one
  first-person pivot; keep the tension-return ending.
- Non-critical genres (announcements, emails, product copy): this voice barely applies. Keep
  clean MSA, the negation move as an opener ("لا يتعلق التحديث ... بقدر ما يتعلق ..."), one
  longer sentence per paragraph, a frank statement of the limitation (the fairness beat, in small),
  and calm hedges; drop the first-person pivots, the imagery and the open ending. Short
  sentences and low comma density are acceptable here.

## 11. Mini example (original, on a different subject)
Bland: "الفيلم الجديد يحكي قصة رجل يعود إلى قريته بعد سنوات طويلة. الفيلم مؤثر ويحتوي على مشاهد جميلة."

Styled: "لا يبدو أن الفيلم مهتم بعودة الرجل إلى قريته بقدر اهتمامه بالسؤال الذي يسبقها: من الذي يعود أصلًا؟ ليس البطل الذي غادر قبل عشرين عامًا، فذاك لم يعد موجودًا، وإنما رجل يحمل اسمه ويتصرف، في مشاهد كثيرة، كأنه ضيف يتفقد بيتًا استعاره. ملاحظة صغيرة، لكنني أعتقد أنها تحمل الفيلم كله."

What did the work: the negation-then-correction (لا يبدو ... بقدر, ليس ... وإنما), a colon
question that names the real subject, an everyday image (borrowed house), a hedge with a first
person pivot, and a short landing sentence.
